## What the researcher changed (verified by me)

  - QK-norm, RoPE and the FP8 cache write inside attention;
  - all 8 query heads of each KV head in one CTA;
  - 64-key tiles;
  - splits balanced by length.
  - It gives no new attention lead. Recorded in TRIED.md.
- **Folding the norm into the producer's last CTA was already closed by inspection** (`decode-fresh/experiments/prepared/decoder_projection_norm_fusion_review.md`): it runs the whole row on one SM and the ceiling is marginal. Dropped. It is replaced by the **consumer-side** norm (C below), as used in Hazy's megakernel.
- **The MoE floor at 32+ tokens is uncertain.** The judge counts 0.75 x the distinct experts (`reference.py:677-688`, `ROUTING_SHARE = 0.75`). Our "~62 us floor" may already include that discount, in which case the real floor could be up to about 83 us. The 5.8-8.8 us tail came from a trace with different resources (`diagnostic-verdict.json`), so it is not proof of a gain. **MoE work at 32+ tokens waits for a diagnostic.**
- **Rejected imports:** Meta lazy pre-norm (breaks the BF16 boundary), POD-Attention (needs mixed prefill), LeanAttention/FA3 swaps (change the reduction order), MonoMoE SwapAB (FP8, 8 tokens or fewer).

## Phase 3: pivot, a persistent FFN island at 1/8 tokens (go/no-go Nov 2)
- **What:** one persistent kernel at 1/8 tokens running R2f (pooler + router), then the norm, then MoE, with static roles and counters instead of kernel boundaries. Extend toward the full layer only if this pays off.
- **Upside:** 12-20 us per layer at 1/8 tokens, 4-7% of Decode. It overlaps with A and C, so the gains don't add.
- **Gates:** M2 (C) must show at least 2 us first; then an island prototype on decode1 sliding must show at least 5 us net, byte-identical, by Nov 2 evening. Otherwise it is dropped.
- **Risk:** very high (registers, shared-memory lifetimes, compiler, bitwise). A full-layer rewrite is unlikely to fit before Nov 4.
