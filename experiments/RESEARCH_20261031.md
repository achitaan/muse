### 1. Kernel boundaries that still run without PDL
- **Which boundaries:**
  - stock norm (attention boundary, and the FFN boundary at 16+);
  - `_sfn` and `_pn` (Triton);
  - SGLang `routing_kernel` (16+);
  - MoE launch.
- **How:**
  - Triton: `tl.extra.cuda.gdc_wait()` before the first load of the producer's output, `gdc_launch_dependents()` at entry, and `launch_pdl=True` at launch. Source: triton-lang tutorial 11 and `third_party/nvidia/language/cuda/gdc.py` (checked).
  - Norm: a verbatim copy of the pinned scalar SGLang kernel (`results/decoder-cluster-norm-reduction-diagnosis-20261029/pinned-upstream/norm_residual.cuh`), keeping 256 logical threads, the vec8 packs and the 8-warp XOR tree, so the output is bitwise identical. Add a PDL preamble that loads the norm weights and the residual stream (both produced earlier) before `griddepcontrol.wait`.
  - MoE: `okc_set_pdl(1)` exists. It needs `_pn` to trigger early, and possibly some setup moved above the wait.
- **Expected:** Hazy measured about 1.3 us graph launch per kernel on H100 (checked). Expect 0.5-1 us per boundary, 4-5 boundaries per layer.
- **Gate problem:** each piece alone is about 1 us, below the 3% complete-layer gate. Bundle them, or lower the threshold for paired in-prepare A/B (see Process).

### 2. Norm folded into the producer's last CTA
- **Targets:**
  - o GEMV (via an atomic counter): the last CTA runs the exact scalar norm on the row while the next kernel preloads under PDL;
  - unpooler slice (1-8 tokens): the last CTA runs the `_sfn` math.
- **Saves:** the whole boundary, about 2.5 us each, not just its launch.
- **Constraint:** the reduction order must stay bitwise. A CPU model of the SGLang order already exists. `_sfn` is Triton, so its order must be replicated or proven.
- **At 32 tokens:** the "last k CTAs take one row each" ranking is needed.
- **Precedent:** Hazy megakernel `rms_*` instructions and MPK both fuse the norm into neighbouring ops (`hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2025-05-27-no-bubbles`, arXiv 2512.22219).

### 3. Top-k routing inside the 9-256-token router kernels
- R2f already does this at 1/8 tokens.
- The `sk2` kernel at 16/32 and `rwdl` at 128/256 emit logits, then a separate 2.7-3.1 us `routing_kernel` runs.
- A last-CTA top-8 with the exact SGLang tie semantics (reusing R2f's finalizer) would save about 2 us per layer at 16-256 tokens.

### 4. MoE at 17-256 tokens: readiness flags instead of the grid barrier
- Barrier removal was closed only for small M, where the tail is 1.6-2.5 us.
- At M = 32-256 the trace shows a 5.8-8.8 us tail and 7.7 us from end of phase B to the join.
- Per-expert flags, as in MonoMoE (arXiv 2609.04244, checked), would let finalization proceed per token as its experts finish.
- MID vs MAIN at 32 tokens is a tie (410.5 vs 413.4 us, `63c17c9c`), so the token layout is not the lever.
- MonoMoE caveat (checked): it targets 8 tokens or fewer, with FP8 W8A8. Its SwapAB layout doesn't transfer to 32 tokens: masked N=32 MMA costs about 42 GFLOP, roughly 42 us.

### 5. R2f at 1/8 tokens (19.2 / 18.0 vs 13.7 floor)
- Router-first CTA order is still untested.
- The resource-isolation control (router sidecar) is closed: 30-34% slower (other session).

### 6. Attention
- **Sliding:**
  - folding the finalize (normalize, RMSNorm x gate) into the o GEMV prologue would remove about 0.8-1.1 us plus a boundary;
  - FlashDecoding++'s unified-max idea is already in place (fixed reference 2^0).
- **Full context:** 1.12-1.16x of the KV floor with page-size-1 scattered rows.

### 7. Large-batch dense
cuBLAS BF16 nvjet takes 45 us for pooler+unpooler at verify16, against about 31 us for BF16 at 4.3 TB/s and about 23 us with c12. The dense-top50 track owns this.

## Process
- The 3% complete-layer gate cannot see real 1-2 us wins: 1 us is 1.3% of decode1. The other session parked its M1 norm-PDL draft for exactly this reason.
- In-prepare paired A/B with both arm orders has far less noise than the 3.4% between separate checks.
- **Options:**
  - gate a bundle of small changes jointly at 3%;
  - or gate each at, say, at least 1% plus agreement in both arm orders.
- This needs the user's decision.

## Realistic total
- #1-#4 landing: about +4-6%, giving about 2.16-2.20x.
- The top 50 needs about 2.25x and is still moving.
