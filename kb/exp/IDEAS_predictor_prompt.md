## Make an honest score predictor

1. Reuse existing parsers and the supplied muse_score.py if present. Historical raw jobs are under cf36b14:experiments/top50/profiles and the two newer complete records are in kb/runs. Support both formats. The score calculator reproduced all 44 passing jobs in the old archive plus the two newer checks. For each of six loads, divide sum(weight * baseline_ns) by sum(weight * median_ns), using the judge's adjusted fields. Take the geometric mean across loads. Decode has 46 sliding and 16 full layers in these records. Reject incomplete inputs and replay a saved score before trusting the parser.
2. Build rows keyed by board, evaluator, time, source hash, parent, actual adopted path, kernel family and shape. Keep failures, no-adoptions, stale/warm/write-flush diagnostics, withdrawn scores and clean judge measurements distinct. A fallback result labels the incumbent, not the failed arm. Compile failures can inform feasibility. Dedupe jobs and equivalent codegen. Treat one run's cases and repeats as correlated.
3. Keep two questions separate. Before ANY timing, use features known from code/compile or parent history: touched stage and loads, tile sizes, split-K, resources, required HBM bytes, route-skew assumptions, and operation overlap. After a local paired A/B, add the exposed full-layer timing deltas as a distinct, better-informed forecast. Never feed a candidate's official timings, gates decided after the prediction, score, or job identifiers into the pre-measurement model.
4. Start with the unchanged-parent predictor and a critical-path estimate per shape. Fit a small regularized model of log(candidate_latency / parent_latency) only where matched data support it. Compare it against assuming zero transfer from an isolated win and against assuming full transfer. Group siblings and identical paths, hold out later revisions, and test the old-to-Keell rebase as a separate shift. Report sample count, absolute score error, sign accuracy beyond observed noise, and top-3 selection quality on historically comparable candidate sets. Only report calibrated probabilities or prediction intervals if coverage on genuinely held-out jobs supports them. Otherwise give conditional ranges or abstain.
5. Use the existing Python prototype as a regression test. It trained a two-probe ridge model on 29 old jobs in 22 revision groups and held out 14 jobs in 9 later groups. Its group-weighted mean absolute error was 0.01075x vs 0.01112x for the same two known loads plus a constant for the other four. It underpredicted newer Alygg and Keell checks by 0.05610x and 0.07859x. It sees two measured load ratios, so it cannot establish source-only prediction. Do not promote it into a live decision rule. A useful negative result is acceptable.
6. Filter impossible or invalid variants before ranking. Shared memory, register/launch limits, and required precision are hard constraints when proven. For timing floors, state compulsory bytes, reuse assumptions, traffic level and hardware ceiling. A measured copy benchmark is an empirical comparator. Enumerate feasible one-change neighbors and a few compatible two-change variants of the existing kernel; introduce genetic search only if it improves offline ranking per evaluation. Compare against random or nearest-incumbent selection where the archive supports the comparison.
